# Candy Crowd 3D style guide

## Visual DNA

Playful grainy 3D editorial character system with distinct candy-colored adult silhouettes, tactile matte materials, and one clear group interaction about community, learning, or teamwork.

- Aspect ratio: 16:9 horizontal
- Canvas: soft lilac, pale blue, or clean studio field with a coherent ground plane and a compact group composition
- Rendering language: rounded sculpted forms, fine visible grain, soft matte shading, simplified facial features, varied silhouettes, and gentle cinematic depth
- Minimum whitespace: 23%
- Maximum subject coverage: 73%
- Typography: No letters, numbers, clothing marks, interface icons, logos, or pseudo-writing.

## Palette

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette 1 | `#A596E8` | primary structure and focal subject |
| Palette 2 | `#A9D96E` | supporting contrast and secondary forms |
| Palette 3 | `#EF686A` | accent for emphasis and directional cues |
| Palette 4 | `#F0CD50` | restrained supporting accent |

## Must

- three to six distinct characters
- one group action
- adult body language
- coherent candy material family
- clear foreground and background roles

## Never

- toy advertisement
- mascot pack
- children's television
- identical avatars
- glossy plastic
- random character crowd

## Negative constraints

- no text
- no logo
- no oversized baby heads
- no branded toy resemblance
- no extra limbs
- no emoji faces

## Composition patterns

### Focal metaphor

Best for: core argument, abstract concept.

Build one dominant physical metaphor around the article's central relationship.

### Contrast

Best for: before and after, tradeoff, tension.

Use two clearly different states joined by one meaningful transition.

### Process

Best for: workflow, cause and effect, transformation.

Show only the essential stages and make the change itself visually dominant.

### System relationship

Best for: framework, network, components.

Limit the system to three to five meaningful parts with an obvious hierarchy.

### Narrative scene

Best for: human consequence, decision, editorial tension.

Stage one decisive moment rather than summarizing the whole article.

## Metaphor method

1. Translate the abstract relationship into a physical arrangement.
2. Choose one object family suited to the visual medium.
3. Make the decisive change visible without relying on a headline.

Object pool:

- shared structure
- learning circle
- community platform
- soft bridge
- common garden
- assembly table
- colorful shelter
- collective instrument

Action pool:

- learn
- welcome
- build
- share
- explore
- cooperate
- teach
- celebrate

Anti-repetition:

- Change both the main object and the spatial arrangement between illustrations.
- Do not repeat the same composition pattern consecutively.
- Derive metaphors from the current article rather than a stock visual library.

## QA checklist

- characters are visually distinct
- interaction tells the story
- materials feel tactile
- group hierarchy is clear
- result avoids toy-pack styling

Failure signals:

- looks like a toy catalog
- characters are interchangeable
- group has no action
- plastic gloss dominates
- anatomy is malformed
