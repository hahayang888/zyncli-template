# Orange Impact style guide

## Visual DNA

High-contrast conceptual editorial illustration using saturated safety orange, black silhouettes, off-white fragments, hard cropping, and one immediate visual thesis.

- Aspect ratio: 16:9 horizontal
- Canvas: large controlled orange or black field with subtle screen-print grain and one dominant collision or threshold
- Rendering language: hard flat silhouettes, decisive crops, minimal forms, rough print texture, and no illustrative detail that weakens the thesis
- Minimum whitespace: 28%
- Maximum subject coverage: 70%
- Typography: No headline, slogan, campaign copy, symbol system, or pseudo-type.

## Palette

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette 1 | `#11100F` | primary structure and focal subject |
| Palette 2 | `#FF4B0A` | supporting contrast and secondary forms |
| Palette 3 | `#F3E9D6` | accent for emphasis and directional cues |

## Must

- one-second comprehension
- two dominant forms at most
- one small decisive intervention
- orange with a semantic role
- strong scale contrast
- original object relationship

## Never

- sale banner
- sports poster
- propaganda imitation
- hazard-tape cliché
- campaign typography
- many icons
- glossy 3D
- decorative orange palette

## Negative constraints

- no text
- no logo
- no poster title
- no UI
- no robot
- no hazard stripes
- no advertisement polish
- no extra decorative objects

## Composition patterns

### Focal metaphor

Best for: core argument, abstract concept.

Build one dominant physical metaphor around the article's central relationship.

### Contrast

Best for: before and after, tradeoff, tension.

Use two clearly different states joined by one meaningful transition.

### Process

Best for: workflow, cause and effect, transformation.

Show only the essential stages and make the change itself visually dominant.

### System relationship

Best for: framework, network, components.

Limit the system to three to five meaningful parts with an obvious hierarchy.

### Narrative scene

Best for: human consequence, decision, editorial tension.

Stage one decisive moment rather than summarizing the whole article.

## Metaphor method

1. Translate the abstract relationship into a physical arrangement.
2. Choose one object family suited to the visual medium.
3. Make the decisive change visible without relying on a headline.

Object pool:

- comb
- barrier
- press
- gate
- wedge
- lever
- channel
- counterweight

Action pool:

- interrupt
- lift
- block
- separate
- compress
- cut through
- redirect
- resist

Anti-repetition:

- Change both the main object and the spatial arrangement between illustrations.
- Do not repeat the same composition pattern consecutively.
- Derive metaphors from the current article rather than a stock visual library.

## QA checklist

- silhouette reads at thumbnail size
- orange and black have clear hierarchy
- one visual thesis dominates
- crop feels intentional
- image is concept-first rather than palette-first

Failure signals:

- looks like an ad
- poster cliché
- too many repeated symbols
- orange is merely decorative
- weak focal intervention
- message requires a caption
