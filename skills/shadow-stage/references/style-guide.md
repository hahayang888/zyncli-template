# Shadow Stage style guide

## Visual DNA

Handcrafted multi-layer black-paper shadow theatre in which fragmented silhouettes align into one bridge or passage through a controlled cobalt backlight.

- Aspect ratio: 16:9 horizontal
- Canvas: deep matte stage box with visible paper layers, one cobalt light source, and a quiet black perimeter
- Rendering language: hand-cut silhouette edges, layered paper depth, subtle fiber, controlled cast shadows, and sparse pale-blue highlights
- Minimum whitespace: 31%
- Maximum subject coverage: 64%
- Typography: No text, title cards, theatrical lettering, logos, or projected words.

## Palette

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette 1 | `#10151E` | primary structure and focal subject |
| Palette 2 | `#1746B7` | supporting contrast and secondary forms |
| Palette 3 | `#7899E5` | accent for emphasis and directional cues |
| Palette 4 | `#E3DED3` | restrained supporting accent |

## Must

- at least three physical depth layers
- one alignment event
- cobalt light has a clear source
- silhouettes remain readable
- hand-cut material is visible

## Never

- horror silhouette
- film-noir still
- children's puppet show
- fantasy castle
- flat vector shadow
- stage curtains

## Negative constraints

- no text
- no logo
- no gore
- no facial detail
- no neon rainbow
- no cinematic lens effects
- no decorative stars

## Composition patterns

### Focal metaphor

Best for: core argument, abstract concept.

Build one dominant physical metaphor around the article's central relationship.

### Contrast

Best for: before and after, tradeoff, tension.

Use two clearly different states joined by one meaningful transition.

### Process

Best for: workflow, cause and effect, transformation.

Show only the essential stages and make the change itself visually dominant.

### System relationship

Best for: framework, network, components.

Limit the system to three to five meaningful parts with an obvious hierarchy.

### Narrative scene

Best for: human consequence, decision, editorial tension.

Stage one decisive moment rather than summarizing the whole article.

## Metaphor method

1. Translate the abstract relationship into a physical arrangement.
2. Choose one object family suited to the visual medium.
3. Make the decisive change visible without relying on a headline.

Object pool:

- layered bridge
- shadow threshold
- aligned figures
- cut-paper machinery
- hidden corridor
- projected route
- fragmented stage
- backlit aperture

Action pool:

- align
- reveal
- bridge
- project
- connect
- cross
- emerge
- conceal

Anti-repetition:

- Change both the main object and the spatial arrangement between illustrations.
- Do not repeat the same composition pattern consecutively.
- Derive metaphors from the current article rather than a stock visual library.

## QA checklist

- depth layers are physically legible
- light reveals the relationship
- silhouette carries meaning
- scene avoids horror
- paper construction feels handcrafted

Failure signals:

- flat digital silhouette
- generic dark scene
- too theatrical
- light source is unclear
- paper texture disappears
