# Connected Rooms style guide

## Visual DNA

Warm modular workplace illustration showing several inhabited rooms linked by purposeful messages, shared objects, or repeated actions across one distributed human system.

- Aspect ratio: 16:9 horizontal
- Canvas: warm ochre or cream field with three to five room modules, one open region, and no browser or presentation frame
- Rendering language: soft flat vector shapes, lightly textured surfaces, simplified credible adults, rounded room corners, and selective fine line details
- Minimum whitespace: 22%
- Maximum subject coverage: 74%
- Typography: No chat text, interface labels, signage, or pseudo-writing; communication is shown through form and direction.

## Palette

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette 1 | `#C69A62` | primary structure and focal subject |
| Palette 2 | `#8A5D62` | supporting contrast and secondary forms |
| Palette 3 | `#E66E50` | accent for emphasis and directional cues |
| Palette 4 | `#F1D39A` | restrained supporting accent |

## Must

- three to five distinct rooms
- one specific activity per room
- one visible cross-room relationship
- credible adult gestures
- shared visual rhythm

## Never

- video-call screenshot
- generic happy remote team
- social network UI
- house cutaway clutter
- identical characters
- decorative message bubbles

## Negative constraints

- no text
- no logo
- no fake UI
- no branded laptop
- no stock teamwork pose
- no unreadable chat bubbles

## Composition patterns

### Focal metaphor

Best for: core argument, abstract concept.

Build one dominant physical metaphor around the article's central relationship.

### Contrast

Best for: before and after, tradeoff, tension.

Use two clearly different states joined by one meaningful transition.

### Process

Best for: workflow, cause and effect, transformation.

Show only the essential stages and make the change itself visually dominant.

### System relationship

Best for: framework, network, components.

Limit the system to three to five meaningful parts with an obvious hierarchy.

### Narrative scene

Best for: human consequence, decision, editorial tension.

Stage one decisive moment rather than summarizing the whole article.

## Metaphor method

1. Translate the abstract relationship into a physical arrangement.
2. Choose one object family suited to the visual medium.
3. Make the decisive change visible without relying on a headline.

Object pool:

- shared shelf
- passing parcel
- open window
- linked worktables
- common instrument
- message thread
- distributed workshop
- relay object

Action pool:

- coordinate
- handoff
- respond
- share
- practice
- teach
- listen
- assemble

Anti-repetition:

- Change both the main object and the spatial arrangement between illustrations.
- Do not repeat the same composition pattern consecutively.
- Derive metaphors from the current article rather than a stock visual library.

## QA checklist

- each room has a narrative job
- cross-room relationship is visible
- characters feel distinct
- warmth preserves hierarchy
- scene avoids UI conventions

Failure signals:

- rooms become decorative
- activities are generic
- too many small props
- looks like a meeting app
- connection has no meaning
