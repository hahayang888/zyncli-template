# Human Lines style guide

## Visual DNA

Authored hand-drawn editorial illustration using imperfect ink, restrained spot color, human gesture, and one clear surreal metaphor.

- Aspect ratio: 16:9 horizontal
- Canvas: warm uncoated paper with generous quiet space, faint ink variation, and no decorative scene filling
- Rendering language: variable imperfect ink line, selective dry-brush texture, loose but intentional anatomy, and flat spot-color shapes that may drift slightly out of register
- Minimum whitespace: 35%
- Maximum subject coverage: 61%
- Typography: No headline. A single handwritten functional word is allowed only when the concept cannot work without it.

## Palette

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette 1 | `#1E2430` | primary structure and focal subject |
| Palette 2 | `#E85C4A` | supporting contrast and secondary forms |
| Palette 3 | `#5C84C6` | accent for emphasis and directional cues |
| Palette 4 | `#F5F0E7` | restrained supporting accent |

## Must

- one emotionally precise human action
- one surprising but legible metaphor
- visible hand-drawn variation
- restrained black plus one or two spot colors
- editorial ambiguity with a clear core idea
- a composition that feels authored

## Never

- cute mascot
- children's-book rendering
- generic flat-vector character
- perfect digital outline
- watercolor wash everywhere
- comic panel
- decorative doodle cloud
- photorealistic anatomy

## Negative constraints

- no mascot proportions
- no stock vector style
- no polished corporate illustration
- no text block
- no speech bubble
- no comic border
- no dense crosshatching
- no sentimental cliché
- notes and paper surfaces contain no writing-like marks

## Composition patterns

### Focal metaphor

Best for: core argument, abstract concept.

Build one dominant physical metaphor around the article's central relationship.

### Contrast

Best for: before and after, tradeoff, tension.

Use two clearly different states joined by one meaningful transition.

### Process

Best for: workflow, cause and effect, transformation.

Show only the essential stages and make the change itself visually dominant.

### System relationship

Best for: framework, network, components.

Limit the system to three to five meaningful parts with an obvious hierarchy.

### Narrative scene

Best for: human consequence, decision, editorial tension.

Stage one decisive moment rather than summarizing the whole article.

## Metaphor method

1. Translate the abstract relationship into a physical arrangement.
2. Choose one object family suited to the visual medium.
3. Make the decisive change visible without relying on a headline.

Object pool:

- unspooled thread
- portable horizon
- paper shadow
- open ribcage as room
- misaligned ladder
- folded landscape
- borrowed umbrella
- small weather system
- door carried by a person
- floating weight

Action pool:

- carry
- unravel
- shelter
- release
- lean
- repair
- step through
- listen
- hold apart
- grow around

Anti-repetition:

- Change both the main object and the spatial arrangement between illustrations.
- Do not repeat the same composition pattern consecutively.
- Derive metaphors from the current article rather than a stock visual library.

## QA checklist

- metaphor is fresh and article-specific
- line quality feels genuinely hand drawn
- spot color has a conceptual role
- emotion comes through gesture
- whitespace remains generous
- does not resemble a mascot or stock vector

Failure signals:

- generic self-care symbolism
- cute cartoon tone
- digital line is too perfect
- metaphor is obscure
- color is decorative
- scene contains too many doodles
- paper props contain pseudo-text
