# Social Pulse style guide

## Visual DNA

Bold community editorial illustration with one expressive participant, a small orbit of social signals, and saturated flat color organized around a single act of participation.

- Aspect ratio: 16:9 horizontal
- Canvas: one saturated warm field or clean cream field with a circular or diagonal energy path and a grounded central figure
- Rendering language: confident flat shapes, simplified adult anatomy, subtle screen-print texture, high color contrast, and minimal outlined details
- Minimum whitespace: 24%
- Maximum subject coverage: 72%
- Typography: No platform names, handles, comments, slogans, app interfaces, or pseudo-writing.

## Palette

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette 1 | `#F4A62A` | primary structure and focal subject |
| Palette 2 | `#2455C3` | supporting contrast and secondary forms |
| Palette 3 | `#E4483B` | accent for emphasis and directional cues |
| Palette 4 | `#279477` | restrained supporting accent |

## Must

- one specific participation action
- one dominant adult figure
- three to six supporting social signals
- clear circular or directional rhythm
- platform-neutral symbols

## Never

- influencer advertisement
- generic like-icon cloud
- phone UI screenshot
- brand imitation
- celebrity likeness
- floating icon overload

## Negative constraints

- no text
- no logo
- no platform branding
- no fake UI
- no public figure
- no excessive reaction icons

## Composition patterns

### Focal metaphor

Best for: core argument, abstract concept.

Build one dominant physical metaphor around the article's central relationship.

### Contrast

Best for: before and after, tradeoff, tension.

Use two clearly different states joined by one meaningful transition.

### Process

Best for: workflow, cause and effect, transformation.

Show only the essential stages and make the change itself visually dominant.

### System relationship

Best for: framework, network, components.

Limit the system to three to five meaningful parts with an obvious hierarchy.

### Narrative scene

Best for: human consequence, decision, editorial tension.

Stage one decisive moment rather than summarizing the whole article.

## Metaphor method

1. Translate the abstract relationship into a physical arrangement.
2. Choose one object family suited to the visual medium.
3. Make the decisive change visible without relying on a headline.

Object pool:

- shared pulse
- response orbit
- community mirror
- signal garden
- participation loop
- audience bridge
- feedback field
- common thread

Action pool:

- respond
- amplify
- invite
- share
- contribute
- moderate
- gather
- reciprocate

Anti-repetition:

- Change both the main object and the spatial arrangement between illustrations.
- Do not repeat the same composition pattern consecutively.
- Derive metaphors from the current article rather than a stock visual library.

## QA checklist

- action is specific
- symbols support rather than dominate
- color hierarchy remains legible
- figure feels original
- image is platform neutral

Failure signals:

- generic social media art
- too many icons
- scene resembles an ad
- unclear focal action
- palette becomes chaotic
