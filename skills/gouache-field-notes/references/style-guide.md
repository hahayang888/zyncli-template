# Gouache Field Notes style guide

## Visual DNA

Observational editorial gouache with matte opaque color, simplified credible adults, pinned research material, and visible brush decisions focused on one act of inquiry.

- Aspect ratio: 16:9 horizontal
- Canvas: warm uncoated paper or a compact real workspace with broad quiet areas and no decorative border
- Rendering language: opaque gouache blocks, dry-brush edges, selective pencil construction, simplified anatomy, and softly flattened perspective
- Minimum whitespace: 22%
- Maximum subject coverage: 74%
- Typography: No headline; reference papers remain blank or carry at most three supplied short labels in hand-painted block lettering.

## Palette

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette 1 | `#234A67` | primary structure and focal subject |
| Palette 2 | `#C76143` | supporting contrast and secondary forms |
| Palette 3 | `#D6B86A` | accent for emphasis and directional cues |
| Palette 4 | `#E9E1D2` | restrained supporting accent |

## Must

- one specific research action
- credible adult gesture
- visible painted surface
- evidence arranged with purpose
- warm-cool color hierarchy

## Never

- teamwork stock pose
- children's-book faces
- corporate meeting
- digital vector finish
- decorative stationery
- dense fake writing

## Negative constraints

- no brand logos
- no photorealism
- no smiling at camera
- no presentation screen
- no illegible pseudo-text
- no generic office icons

## Composition patterns

### Focal metaphor

Best for: core argument, abstract concept.

Build one dominant physical metaphor around the article's central relationship.

### Contrast

Best for: before and after, tradeoff, tension.

Use two clearly different states joined by one meaningful transition.

### Process

Best for: workflow, cause and effect, transformation.

Show only the essential stages and make the change itself visually dominant.

### System relationship

Best for: framework, network, components.

Limit the system to three to five meaningful parts with an obvious hierarchy.

### Narrative scene

Best for: human consequence, decision, editorial tension.

Stage one decisive moment rather than summarizing the whole article.

## Metaphor method

1. Translate the abstract relationship into a physical arrangement.
2. Choose one object family suited to the visual medium.
3. Make the decisive change visible without relying on a headline.

Object pool:

- evidence wall
- field notebook
- sample table
- map fragments
- pinned studies
- shared model
- observation window
- test arrangement

Action pool:

- observe
- compare
- pin
- sort
- test
- trace
- discuss
- revise

Anti-repetition:

- Change both the main object and the spatial arrangement between illustrations.
- Do not repeat the same composition pattern consecutively.
- Derive metaphors from the current article rather than a stock visual library.

## QA checklist

- human action proves the idea
- brush texture remains visible
- evidence is legible as material
- scene avoids stock staging
- focal gesture is clear

Failure signals:

- generic brainstorming
- cute character style
- too many props
- flat vector look
- evidence becomes meaningless decoration
