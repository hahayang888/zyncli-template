# Inclusive Monochrome style guide

## Visual DNA

Human-centered black-and-white editorial line illustration with confident fills, credible varied adult bodies, open white space, and one specific collaborative action.

- Aspect ratio: 16:9 horizontal
- Canvas: pure white field with large open margins and only the structural surface needed for the interaction
- Rendering language: crisp variable black outlines, selective solid-black clothing and object fills, simplified adult anatomy, and no gray shading or spot color
- Minimum whitespace: 32%
- Maximum subject coverage: 68%
- Typography: No headline, labels, signs, pseudo-writing, clothing marks, or interface text.

## Palette

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette 1 | `#111111` | primary structure and focal subject |
| Palette 2 | `#FFFFFF` | supporting contrast and secondary forms |

## Must

- one specific human interaction
- three to five adults maximum
- body language that explains the idea
- natural varied representation
- clear silhouettes
- editorial rather than promotional tone

## Never

- diversity lineup
- corporate brochure
- generic smiling team
- exaggerated representation
- cute cartoon proportions
- floating business icons
- stock-vector celebration
- motivational poster

## Negative constraints

- no text
- no logo
- no color
- no gray gradient
- no UI
- no lineup pose
- no decorative icons
- no corporate stock scene

## Composition patterns

### Focal metaphor

Best for: core argument, abstract concept.

Build one dominant physical metaphor around the article's central relationship.

### Contrast

Best for: before and after, tradeoff, tension.

Use two clearly different states joined by one meaningful transition.

### Process

Best for: workflow, cause and effect, transformation.

Show only the essential stages and make the change itself visually dominant.

### System relationship

Best for: framework, network, components.

Limit the system to three to five meaningful parts with an obvious hierarchy.

### Narrative scene

Best for: human consequence, decision, editorial tension.

Stage one decisive moment rather than summarizing the whole article.

## Metaphor method

1. Translate the abstract relationship into a physical arrangement.
2. Choose one object family suited to the visual medium.
3. Make the decisive change visible without relying on a headline.

Object pool:

- routing table
- shared object
- adjustable bridge
- collective counterweight
- open threshold
- handoff rail
- modular seat
- shared tool

Action pool:

- handoff
- listen
- make room
- coordinate
- redirect
- support
- negotiate
- include

Anti-repetition:

- Change both the main object and the spatial arrangement between illustrations.
- Do not repeat the same composition pattern consecutively.
- Derive metaphors from the current article rather than a stock visual library.

## QA checklist

- gesture explains the relationship
- representation feels natural
- scene is specific rather than symbolic tokenism
- black fills remain balanced
- white space stays open
- no stock-corporate cliché

Failure signals:

- people posed in a lineup
- generic happy team
- representation overwhelms the article idea
- too many characters
- flat stock-vector polish
- weak interaction
