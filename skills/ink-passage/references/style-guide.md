# Ink Passage style guide

## Visual DNA

Contemporary ink-wash editorial abstraction where fragments, diluted fields, and one architectural passage make uncertainty and transition navigable.

- Aspect ratio: 16:9 horizontal
- Canvas: warm absorbent paper with expansive untouched areas, one pooled black field, and a quiet horizontal or vertical route
- Rendering language: wet-on-wet ink, dry-brush architecture, diluted cobalt atmosphere, and one restrained coral directional cue
- Minimum whitespace: 35%
- Maximum subject coverage: 60%
- Typography: No text, calligraphy, seals, pseudo-Asian lettering, or decorative signatures.

## Palette

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette 1 | `#1C2229` | primary structure and focal subject |
| Palette 2 | `#315A93` | supporting contrast and secondary forms |
| Palette 3 | `#C45D4C` | accent for emphasis and directional cues |
| Palette 4 | `#E9E5DC` | restrained supporting accent |

## Must

- one passage or threshold
- ink density carries hierarchy
- edges vary naturally
- one directional cue
- quiet field remains active

## Never

- traditional landscape imitation
- zen cliché
- calligraphy poster
- random ink splash
- fantasy temple
- decorative watercolor wash

## Negative constraints

- no text
- no red seal
- no logo
- no ornamental border
- no detailed architecture
- no rainbow watercolor

## Composition patterns

### Focal metaphor

Best for: core argument, abstract concept.

Build one dominant physical metaphor around the article's central relationship.

### Contrast

Best for: before and after, tradeoff, tension.

Use two clearly different states joined by one meaningful transition.

### Process

Best for: workflow, cause and effect, transformation.

Show only the essential stages and make the change itself visually dominant.

### System relationship

Best for: framework, network, components.

Limit the system to three to five meaningful parts with an obvious hierarchy.

### Narrative scene

Best for: human consequence, decision, editorial tension.

Stage one decisive moment rather than summarizing the whole article.

## Metaphor method

1. Translate the abstract relationship into a physical arrangement.
2. Choose one object family suited to the visual medium.
3. Make the decisive change visible without relying on a headline.

Object pool:

- ink threshold
- washed corridor
- broken wall
- floating plane
- narrow bridge
- open chamber
- dissolving edge
- quiet shelter

Action pool:

- pass
- emerge
- dissolve
- cross
- open
- gather
- separate
- settle

Anti-repetition:

- Change both the main object and the spatial arrangement between illustrations.
- Do not repeat the same composition pattern consecutively.
- Derive metaphors from the current article rather than a stock visual library.

## QA checklist

- route is immediately visible
- ink behavior feels physical
- ambiguity supports meaning
- white space is intentional
- style avoids cultural pastiche

Failure signals:

- generic ink splash
- overly traditional scene
- passage is unclear
- paper is overfilled
- accent color becomes decorative
