# Riso Argument style guide

## Visual DNA

Three-ink risograph editorial graphics that stage one conflict, overlap, or synthesis through coarse paper grain and deliberate registration shift.

- Aspect ratio: 16:9 horizontal
- Canvas: uncoated cream paper with visible ink absorption, generous margins, and no poster border
- Rendering language: flat navy, fluorescent red-orange, and ochre layers with halftone, overprint, rough registration, and bold simple silhouettes
- Minimum whitespace: 26%
- Maximum subject coverage: 69%
- Typography: No headline; supplied labels are limited to three short words set in plain block type with deliberate registration.

## Palette

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette 1 | `#153D76` | primary structure and focal subject |
| Palette 2 | `#F04E3E` | supporting contrast and secondary forms |
| Palette 3 | `#E7B83E` | accent for emphasis and directional cues |
| Palette 4 | `#EEE8DC` | restrained supporting accent |

## Must

- three-ink limit
- one conflict or synthesis
- overprint carries meaning
- bold silhouette
- print error remains controlled

## Never

- retro poster imitation
- comic panel
- Memphis pattern
- random ink speckles
- screen-print slogan
- decorative vintage badge

## Negative constraints

- no logo
- no invented slogan
- no gradients
- no photorealism
- no more than three inks plus paper
- no dense text

## Composition patterns

### Focal metaphor

Best for: core argument, abstract concept.

Build one dominant physical metaphor around the article's central relationship.

### Contrast

Best for: before and after, tradeoff, tension.

Use two clearly different states joined by one meaningful transition.

### Process

Best for: workflow, cause and effect, transformation.

Show only the essential stages and make the change itself visually dominant.

### System relationship

Best for: framework, network, components.

Limit the system to three to five meaningful parts with an obvious hierarchy.

### Narrative scene

Best for: human consequence, decision, editorial tension.

Stage one decisive moment rather than summarizing the whole article.

## Metaphor method

1. Translate the abstract relationship into a physical arrangement.
2. Choose one object family suited to the visual medium.
3. Make the decisive change visible without relying on a headline.

Object pool:

- opposing blocks
- overprinted bridge
- split field
- shared vessel
- compressed channel
- two-part mechanism
- counterweight
- merged signal

Action pool:

- oppose
- overlap
- merge
- press
- interrupt
- resolve
- exchange
- redirect

Anti-repetition:

- Change both the main object and the spatial arrangement between illustrations.
- Do not repeat the same composition pattern consecutively.
- Derive metaphors from the current article rather than a stock visual library.

## QA checklist

- argument reads in one second
- overprint is functional
- ink count is disciplined
- registration feels intentional
- composition is not nostalgic decoration

Failure signals:

- looks like a concert poster
- too many textures
- colors lack hierarchy
- no clear conflict
- misregistration obscures the idea
