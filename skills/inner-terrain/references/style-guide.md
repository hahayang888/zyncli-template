# Inner Terrain style guide

## Visual DNA

Restrained conceptual double-exposure photography in which one anonymous human silhouette contains a coherent natural landscape that expresses an inner state, memory, or relationship to place.

- Aspect ratio: 16:9 horizontal
- Canvas: quiet warm-gray or pale mineral field with one isolated silhouette, soft atmospheric edges, and generous negative space
- Rendering language: photoreal landscape texture, monochrome human boundary, delicate tonal masking, soft film grain, and seamless photographic exposure blending
- Minimum whitespace: 30%
- Maximum subject coverage: 66%
- Typography: No words, signs, labels, logos, or pseudo-writing inside the image.

## Palette

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette 1 | `#D9D8D2` | primary structure and focal subject |
| Palette 2 | `#738C79` | supporting contrast and secondary forms |
| Palette 3 | `#B9C9C7` | accent for emphasis and directional cues |
| Palette 4 | `#303538` | restrained supporting accent |

## Must

- one anonymous silhouette
- one geographically coherent inner landscape
- a meaningful boundary between person and place
- restrained tonal hierarchy
- editorial rather than advertising mood

## Never

- celebrity likeness
- wellness advertisement
- surreal body horror
- multiple unrelated landscapes
- fantasy portal
- stock meditation pose

## Negative constraints

- no text
- no logo
- no recognizable public figure
- no glossy campaign retouching
- no floating planet
- no impossible anatomy

## Composition patterns

### Focal metaphor

Best for: core argument, abstract concept.

Build one dominant physical metaphor around the article's central relationship.

### Contrast

Best for: before and after, tradeoff, tension.

Use two clearly different states joined by one meaningful transition.

### Process

Best for: workflow, cause and effect, transformation.

Show only the essential stages and make the change itself visually dominant.

### System relationship

Best for: framework, network, components.

Limit the system to three to five meaningful parts with an obvious hierarchy.

### Narrative scene

Best for: human consequence, decision, editorial tension.

Stage one decisive moment rather than summarizing the whole article.

## Metaphor method

1. Translate the abstract relationship into a physical arrangement.
2. Choose one object family suited to the visual medium.
3. Make the decisive change visible without relying on a headline.

Object pool:

- forest interior
- river bend
- cloud layer
- coastal horizon
- mountain path
- weather front
- open field
- eroded valley

Action pool:

- contain
- remember
- open
- weather
- recover
- carry
- root
- reconcile

Anti-repetition:

- Change both the main object and the spatial arrangement between illustrations.
- Do not repeat the same composition pattern consecutively.
- Derive metaphors from the current article rather than a stock visual library.

## QA checklist

- double exposure is seamless
- landscape supports the article idea
- silhouette remains anatomically credible
- mood is quiet and specific
- image reads at thumbnail size

Failure signals:

- looks like a stock wellness poster
- masking is visibly broken
- landscape is random
- portrait becomes identifiable
- too many exposure layers
