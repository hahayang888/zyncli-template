# Editorial Portrait style guide

## Visual DNA

Cinematic environmental portrait photography using an anonymous adult, subtle gesture, meaningful context, restrained color, and directional natural light to express a human idea without advertising polish.

- Aspect ratio: 16:9 horizontal
- Canvas: a real room, studio, threshold, street edge, archive, or workplace chosen for narrative relevance, with a calm background and intentional negative space
- Rendering language: photorealistic editorial portraiture, 50mm to 85mm lens language, authentic skin and fabric texture, gentle tonal rolloff, subtle film grain, and controlled depth of field
- Minimum whitespace: 25%
- Maximum subject coverage: 70%
- Typography: No readable books, posters, clothing text, screens, signs, labels, captions, or headline.

## Must

- one anonymous adult
- one emotionally precise gesture
- environmental context
- credible face and hands
- directional light
- quiet editorial tension

## Never

- celebrity lookalike
- beauty campaign
- fashion advertisement
- influencer pose
- forced smile
- corporate headshot
- fantasy costume
- dramatic movie-poster grading

## Negative constraints

- no text
- no logo
- no celebrity
- no public figure
- no glamour retouching
- no direct-to-camera sales pose
- no branded clothing
- no malformed anatomy
- no artificial skin

## Composition patterns

### Focal metaphor

Best for: core argument, abstract concept.

Build one dominant physical metaphor around the article's central relationship.

### Contrast

Best for: before and after, tradeoff, tension.

Use two clearly different states joined by one meaningful transition.

### Process

Best for: workflow, cause and effect, transformation.

Show only the essential stages and make the change itself visually dominant.

### System relationship

Best for: framework, network, components.

Limit the system to three to five meaningful parts with an obvious hierarchy.

### Narrative scene

Best for: human consequence, decision, editorial tension.

Stage one decisive moment rather than summarizing the whole article.

## Metaphor method

1. Translate the abstract relationship into a physical arrangement.
2. Choose one object family suited to the visual medium.
3. Make the decisive change visible without relying on a headline.

Object pool:

- window edge
- unfinished object
- stacked material
- mirror fragment
- work table
- empty frame
- open case
- light boundary
- held tool
- suspended thread

Action pool:

- consider
- hold
- turn away
- prepare
- observe
- release
- choose
- wait
- rebuild
- step forward

Anti-repetition:

- Change both the main object and the spatial arrangement between illustrations.
- Do not repeat the same composition pattern consecutively.
- Derive metaphors from the current article rather than a stock visual library.

## QA checklist

- portrait communicates a specific inner state
- identity is original and anonymous
- skin and anatomy are credible
- context supports the idea
- light has narrative purpose
- result is editorial rather than aspirational advertising

Failure signals:

- generic profile photo
- model-like posing
- beauty retouching
- celebrity resemblance
- empty moody lighting
- hands or face look synthetic
