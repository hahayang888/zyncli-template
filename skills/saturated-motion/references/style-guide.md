# Saturated Motion style guide

## Visual DNA

Kinetic editorial illustration built from sweeping saturated color fields, one elongated adult figure, and a strong directional curve expressing momentum, pressure, or creative transformation.

- Aspect ratio: 16:9 horizontal
- Canvas: full-bleed cobalt, magenta, coral, or pale field with one dominant curve and enough quiet area around the figure
- Rendering language: bold flat vector fields, subtle grain, elongated but credible adult anatomy, sharp color blocking, and selective organic foliage or paper fragments
- Minimum whitespace: 20%
- Maximum subject coverage: 76%
- Typography: No headline, slogan, interface copy, logos, or pseudo-writing.

## Palette

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette 1 | `#253BC9` | primary structure and focal subject |
| Palette 2 | `#ED4E89` | supporting contrast and secondary forms |
| Palette 3 | `#43CFB3` | accent for emphasis and directional cues |
| Palette 4 | `#F2C34D` | restrained supporting accent |

## Must

- one dominant movement direction
- one expressive adult figure
- two to four large color fields
- visible source and destination
- saturation with hierarchy

## Never

- fashion advertisement
- psychedelic pattern
- random colorful waves
- dance poster
- generic productivity scene
- many equal characters

## Negative constraints

- no text
- no logo
- no public figure
- no neon glow
- no malformed limbs
- no decorative confetti overload

## Composition patterns

### Focal metaphor

Best for: core argument, abstract concept.

Build one dominant physical metaphor around the article's central relationship.

### Contrast

Best for: before and after, tradeoff, tension.

Use two clearly different states joined by one meaningful transition.

### Process

Best for: workflow, cause and effect, transformation.

Show only the essential stages and make the change itself visually dominant.

### System relationship

Best for: framework, network, components.

Limit the system to three to five meaningful parts with an obvious hierarchy.

### Narrative scene

Best for: human consequence, decision, editorial tension.

Stage one decisive moment rather than summarizing the whole article.

## Metaphor method

1. Translate the abstract relationship into a physical arrangement.
2. Choose one object family suited to the visual medium.
3. Make the decisive change visible without relying on a headline.

Object pool:

- rising curve
- moving platform
- color current
- paper wake
- tilted workspace
- growth channel
- flexible ramp
- creative tide

Action pool:

- accelerate
- ride
- redirect
- push
- adapt
- launch
- flow
- break through

Anti-repetition:

- Change both the main object and the spatial arrangement between illustrations.
- Do not repeat the same composition pattern consecutively.
- Derive metaphors from the current article rather than a stock visual library.

## QA checklist

- direction reads instantly
- figure remains anatomically credible
- color fields have clear hierarchy
- motion serves the article
- scene avoids campaign art

Failure signals:

- color overwhelms meaning
- looks like an ad
- movement has no destination
- figure anatomy breaks
- too many decorative shapes
