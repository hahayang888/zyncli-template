# Cutout Editorial Collage style guide

## Visual DNA

Sophisticated modern editorial collage combining original generated fragments, flat geometric fields, visible paper edges, restrained halftone, and one intelligent juxtaposition.

- Aspect ratio: 16:9 horizontal
- Canvas: cream or quiet flat field with at least one open region surrounding a controlled layered collage
- Rendering language: cut-paper edges, original photographic or painterly fragments, flat navy geometry, rust and muted cyan accents, and subordinate halftone texture
- Minimum whitespace: 25%
- Maximum subject coverage: 70%
- Typography: No publication text, newspaper fragments, labels, letters, numerals, or pseudo-type anywhere.

## Palette

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette 1 | `#21384D` | primary structure and focal subject |
| Palette 2 | `#E7E1D4` | supporting contrast and secondary forms |
| Palette 3 | `#C75C3D` | accent for emphasis and directional cues |
| Palette 4 | `#6CA7A7` | restrained supporting accent |

## Must

- one concept-driven juxtaposition
- one large anchor fragment
- two to four supporting layers
- clear foreground-background hierarchy
- visible material contrast
- original generated source-like fragments

## Never

- recognizable publication image
- identifiable real person
- celebrity face
- found logo
- stock-photo montage
- scrapbook clutter
- fake newspaper
- mood-board grid

## Negative constraints

- no text
- no pseudo-writing
- no logo
- no celebrity
- no recognizable source image
- no stock montage
- no excessive torn edges
- no UI

## Composition patterns

### Focal metaphor

Best for: core argument, abstract concept.

Build one dominant physical metaphor around the article's central relationship.

### Contrast

Best for: before and after, tradeoff, tension.

Use two clearly different states joined by one meaningful transition.

### Process

Best for: workflow, cause and effect, transformation.

Show only the essential stages and make the change itself visually dominant.

### System relationship

Best for: framework, network, components.

Limit the system to three to five meaningful parts with an obvious hierarchy.

### Narrative scene

Best for: human consequence, decision, editorial tension.

Stage one decisive moment rather than summarizing the whole article.

## Metaphor method

1. Translate the abstract relationship into a physical arrangement.
2. Choose one object family suited to the visual medium.
3. Make the decisive change visible without relying on a headline.

Object pool:

- layered profile
- paper portal
- cutaway system
- fragment archive
- scale-shifted hand
- modular image field
- folded horizon
- collage machine

Action pool:

- insert
- reassemble
- reveal
- filter
- layer
- cut through
- reframe
- reconnect

Anti-repetition:

- Change both the main object and the spatial arrangement between illustrations.
- Do not repeat the same composition pattern consecutively.
- Derive metaphors from the current article rather than a stock visual library.

## QA checklist

- juxtaposition communicates a specific thesis
- layers read at article width
- material contrast is visible
- supporting layers remain capped
- no recognizable copyrighted source element appears

Failure signals:

- random stock montage
- too many fragments
- fake newspaper text
- celebrity resemblance
- decorative collage without a thesis
- layers collapse into noise
