# Oil Allegory style guide

## Visual DNA

Contemporary figurative oil allegory in which one restrained adult action—building, carrying, crossing, repairing—embodies the article's moral or strategic tension.

- Aspect ratio: 16:9 horizontal
- Canvas: spacious muted interior or landscape with a low horizon, directional window light, and atmospheric depth
- Rendering language: visible oil brushwork, simplified but credible anatomy, restrained modeling, and edges that move from firm focal detail to lost atmosphere
- Minimum whitespace: 24%
- Maximum subject coverage: 70%
- Typography: No words, captions, signs, or typographic objects inside the painting.

## Palette

| Role | Color | Use |

| Palette 1 | `#273850` | primary structure and focal subject |
| Palette 2 | `#A9573D` | supporting contrast and secondary forms |
| Palette 3 | `#C79C5B` | accent for emphasis and directional cues |
| Palette 4 | `#D8D0BF` | restrained supporting accent |

## Must

- one human action carries the thesis
- symbolic object remains physical
- directional light clarifies consequence
- adult emotional restraint
- painterly depth

## Never

- religious icon imitation
- old-master quotation
- fantasy art
- melodramatic pose
- photographic smoothness
- crowded narrative tableau

## Negative constraints

- no public figure likeness
- no logos
- no text
- no heroic propaganda pose
- no glossy digital painting
- no ornate historical costume

## Composition patterns

### Focal metaphor

Best for: core argument, abstract concept.

Build one dominant physical metaphor around the article's central relationship.

### Contrast

Best for: before and after, tradeoff, tension.

Use two clearly different states joined by one meaningful transition.

### Process

Best for: workflow, cause and effect, transformation.

Show only the essential stages and make the change itself visually dominant.

### System relationship

Best for: framework, network, components.

Limit the system to three to five meaningful parts with an obvious hierarchy.

### Narrative scene

Best for: human consequence, decision, editorial tension.

Stage one decisive moment rather than summarizing the whole article.

## Metaphor method

1. Translate the abstract relationship into a physical arrangement.
2. Choose one object family suited to the visual medium.
3. Make the decisive change visible without relying on a headline.

Object pool:

- unfinished bridge
- weighted frame
- stone threshold
- broken vessel
- shared beam
- raised platform
- open gate
- assembled fragments

Action pool:

- build
- carry
- repair
- cross
- balance
- offer
- lift
- join

Anti-repetition:

- Change both the main object and the spatial arrangement between illustrations.
- Do not repeat the same composition pattern consecutively.
- Derive metaphors from the current article rather than a stock visual library.

## QA checklist

- action expresses the claim
- light has narrative purpose
- symbol avoids cliché
- figure feels credible
- painting remains contemporary editorial

Failure signals:

- generic inspirational art
- historical pastiche
- overly literal symbolism
- stiff anatomy
- dramatic light without meaning
