# Quiet Support style guide

## Visual DNA

Soft human-centered editorial illustration using simplified adult figures, generous open space, and one emotionally precise gesture of care, grief, belonging, or support.

- Aspect ratio: 16:9 horizontal
- Canvas: pale blush, cream, or quiet lavender field with one grounded interaction and large unoccupied space
- Rendering language: soft geometric bodies, selective fine outlines, matte flat color, simplified credible anatomy, and restrained facial detail
- Minimum whitespace: 34%
- Maximum subject coverage: 62%
- Typography: No quotes, wellness slogans, labels, interface marks, or pseudo-writing.

## Palette

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette 1 | `#F3DDD8` | primary structure and focal subject |
| Palette 2 | `#E8A84B` | supporting contrast and secondary forms |
| Palette 3 | `#27345E` | accent for emphasis and directional cues |
| Palette 4 | `#C6746B` | restrained supporting accent |

## Must

- one emotionally exact gesture
- one or two adults
- credible bodily support
- gentle color contrast
- space that preserves dignity

## Never

- forced positivity
- therapy app advertisement
- childish proportions
- generic hug icon
- melodrama
- decorative hearts

## Negative constraints

- no text
- no logo
- no medical claim
- no exaggerated tears
- no emoji
- no malformed hands

## Composition patterns

### Focal metaphor

Best for: core argument, abstract concept.

Build one dominant physical metaphor around the article's central relationship.

### Contrast

Best for: before and after, tradeoff, tension.

Use two clearly different states joined by one meaningful transition.

### Process

Best for: workflow, cause and effect, transformation.

Show only the essential stages and make the change itself visually dominant.

### System relationship

Best for: framework, network, components.

Limit the system to three to five meaningful parts with an obvious hierarchy.

### Narrative scene

Best for: human consequence, decision, editorial tension.

Stage one decisive moment rather than summarizing the whole article.

## Metaphor method

1. Translate the abstract relationship into a physical arrangement.
2. Choose one object family suited to the visual medium.
3. Make the decisive change visible without relying on a headline.

Object pool:

- shared blanket
- held vessel
- quiet bench
- open circle
- protective chair
- steadying rail
- warm lamp
- mended shape

Action pool:

- hold
- listen
- steady
- sit beside
- make room
- carry
- repair
- remain

Anti-repetition:

- Change both the main object and the spatial arrangement between illustrations.
- Do not repeat the same composition pattern consecutively.
- Derive metaphors from the current article rather than a stock visual library.

## QA checklist

- gesture is legible and respectful
- figures feel adult
- negative space supports emotion
- palette is calm
- scene avoids wellness clichés

Failure signals:

- looks like a self-care app
- gesture is generic
- figures are infantilized
- emotion is overperformed
- decorative symbols replace interaction
