# Office Stories style guide

## Visual DNA

Contemporary workplace editorial illustration with simplified adults, expressive body language, clean architectural staging, bold linework, and understated situational humor.

- Aspect ratio: 16:9 horizontal
- Canvas: airy office, home-office, meeting, or hybrid-work setting reduced to only the architectural cues needed for the story
- Rendering language: confident dark outlines, flat matte color, simplified anatomy, purposeful facial marks, and light paper texture
- Minimum whitespace: 24%
- Maximum subject coverage: 72%
- Typography: No headline or motivational slogan. Screens and notes contain blank shapes unless one or two real short labels are essential.

## Palette

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette 1 | `#243B63` | primary structure and focal subject |
| Palette 2 | `#6D83C6` | supporting contrast and secondary forms |
| Palette 3 | `#E8795B` | accent for emphasis and directional cues |
| Palette 4 | `#F4EBDD` | restrained supporting accent |

## Must

- one specific workplace behavior
- body language that carries the argument
- adult characters with varied but natural representation
- a readable relationship between people and space
- one quiet narrative detail
- editorial observation rather than corporate optimism

## Never

- generic smiling team around a laptop
- handshake scene
- isometric office
- faceless corporate clip art
- everyone celebrating
- oversized pie charts
- brand-colored uniforms
- children's cartoon proportions

## Negative constraints

- no stock illustration pose
- no corporate brochure
- no floating business icons
- no fake screen text
- no huge heads
- no exaggerated diversity lineup
- no motivational headline
- no glossy gradients

## Composition patterns

### Focal metaphor

Best for: core argument, abstract concept.

Build one dominant physical metaphor around the article's central relationship.

### Contrast

Best for: before and after, tradeoff, tension.

Use two clearly different states joined by one meaningful transition.

### Process

Best for: workflow, cause and effect, transformation.

Show only the essential stages and make the change itself visually dominant.

### System relationship

Best for: framework, network, components.

Limit the system to three to five meaningful parts with an obvious hierarchy.

### Narrative scene

Best for: human consequence, decision, editorial tension.

Stage one decisive moment rather than summarizing the whole article.

## Metaphor method

1. Translate the abstract relationship into a physical arrangement.
2. Choose one object family suited to the visual medium.
3. Make the decisive change visible without relying on a headline.

Object pool:

- meeting table
- half-open door
- video-call window
- rolling chair
- shared whiteboard
- desk island
- elevator
- calendar wall
- partition
- office plant

Action pool:

- coordinate
- wait
- interrupt
- listen
- handoff
- focus
- negotiate
- withdraw
- reconnect
- make room

Anti-repetition:

- Change both the main object and the spatial arrangement between illustrations.
- Do not repeat the same composition pattern consecutively.
- Derive metaphors from the current article rather than a stock visual library.

## QA checklist

- gesture explains the situation
- setting is specific but uncluttered
- characters feel adult and credible
- scene contains editorial tension
- no corporate stock clichés
- composition works without screen text

Failure signals:

- generic happy-office scene
- stiff mannequin poses
- too many props
- characters lack interaction
- looks like onboarding software art
- relies on unreadable screens
