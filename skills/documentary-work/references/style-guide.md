# Documentary Work style guide

## Visual DNA

Naturalistic documentary workplace photography with anonymous adults, available light, lived-in environments, restrained color, and one observed action that carries the article's argument.

- Aspect ratio: 16:9 horizontal
- Canvas: a credible contemporary workplace, workshop, studio, public interior, or remote-work environment with useful negative space and only contextually believable objects
- Rendering language: photorealistic full-frame editorial photography, 35mm or 50mm lens language, natural skin texture, realistic hands, subtle film grain, and physically coherent available light
- Minimum whitespace: 22%
- Maximum subject coverage: 72%
- Typography: No readable screen content, signs, notes, labels, brand marks, or in-image headline.

## Palette

| Role | Color | Use |

| Palette 1 | `#24313A` | primary structure and focal subject |
| Palette 2 | `#C5A47A` | supporting contrast and secondary forms |
| Palette 3 | `#E7E1D7` | accent for emphasis and directional cues |
| Palette 4 | `#71848B` | restrained supporting accent |

## Must

- one specific candid action
- anonymous non-famous adults
- natural body language
- believable environment
- physically plausible light
- editorial observation rather than promotion

## Never

- posed team lineup
- everyone smiling at a laptop
- handshake
- office stock photo
- lifestyle advertisement
- celebrity resemblance
- perfect showroom office
- artificial diversity tableau

## Negative constraints

- no text
- no logo
- no branded devices
- no celebrity
- no famous-person resemblance
- no glossy commercial retouching
- no fake UI
- no exaggerated smile
- no extra fingers or malformed hands

## Composition patterns

### Focal metaphor

Best for: core argument, abstract concept.

Build one dominant physical metaphor around the article's central relationship.

### Contrast

Best for: before and after, tradeoff, tension.

Use two clearly different states joined by one meaningful transition.

### Process

Best for: workflow, cause and effect, transformation.

Show only the essential stages and make the change itself visually dominant.

### System relationship

Best for: framework, network, components.

Limit the system to three to five meaningful parts with an obvious hierarchy.

### Narrative scene

Best for: human consequence, decision, editorial tension.

Stage one decisive moment rather than summarizing the whole article.

## Metaphor method

1. Translate the abstract relationship into a physical arrangement.
2. Choose one object family suited to the visual medium.
3. Make the decisive change visible without relying on a headline.

Object pool:

- shared work surface
- threshold
- partition
- tool bench
- window reflection
- unfinished prototype
- empty chair
- material sample
- long table
- open doorway

Action pool:

- listen
- handoff
- inspect
- repair
- coordinate
- pause
- decide
- make room
- focus
- reconsider

Anti-repetition:

- Change both the main object and the spatial arrangement between illustrations.
- Do not repeat the same composition pattern consecutively.
- Derive metaphors from the current article rather than a stock visual library.

## QA checklist

- action feels observed rather than posed
- faces and hands are anatomically credible
- environment contributes meaning
- lighting is plausible
- no branding or readable text
- image feels commissioned for an editorial story

Failure signals:

- generic corporate stock photo
- performative collaboration
- overly polished office
- people stare at camera
- malformed anatomy
- context is decorative rather than meaningful
