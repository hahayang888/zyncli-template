# Raw Logic style guide

## Visual DNA

Deadpan editorial drawing using deliberately raw black lines, blunt flat objects, awkward scale, and one strange physical relationship to expose organizational friction or cultural contradiction.

- Aspect ratio: 16:9 horizontal
- Canvas: off-white paper or one pale muted field with large empty regions and no decorative frame
- Rendering language: uneven confident ink contour, sparse solid-black fill, a few muted flat colors, intentionally simple anatomy, and no polished vector finish
- Minimum whitespace: 35%
- Maximum subject coverage: 62%
- Typography: No caption, punchline, labels, speech bubbles, signs, or pseudo-writing.

## Palette

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette 1 | `#171717` | primary structure and focal subject |
| Palette 2 | `#F4F1E8` | supporting contrast and secondary forms |
| Palette 3 | `#D6E6CC` | accent for emphasis and directional cues |
| Palette 4 | `#D35738` | restrained supporting accent |

## Must

- one deadpan physical metaphor
- one visibly awkward relationship
- few blunt objects
- raw but controlled line
- meaning without a caption

## Never

- copied cartoon style
- cute doodle
- corporate clip art
- busy comic panel
- motivational metaphor
- random scribble

## Negative constraints

- no text
- no logo
- no speech bubble
- no meme
- no famous cartoon resemblance
- no polished stock vector

## Composition patterns

### Focal metaphor

Best for: core argument, abstract concept.

Build one dominant physical metaphor around the article's central relationship.

### Contrast

Best for: before and after, tradeoff, tension.

Use two clearly different states joined by one meaningful transition.

### Process

Best for: workflow, cause and effect, transformation.

Show only the essential stages and make the change itself visually dominant.

### System relationship

Best for: framework, network, components.

Limit the system to three to five meaningful parts with an obvious hierarchy.

### Narrative scene

Best for: human consequence, decision, editorial tension.

Stage one decisive moment rather than summarizing the whole article.

## Metaphor method

1. Translate the abstract relationship into a physical arrangement.
2. Choose one object family suited to the visual medium.
3. Make the decisive change visible without relying on a headline.

Object pool:

- impossible tool
- overloaded handle
- shared suitcase
- crooked ladder
- leaking system
- misfit container
- many-cup spout
- backward lever

Action pool:

- carry
- spill
- misroute
- stretch
- balance
- squeeze
- delegate
- improvise

Anti-repetition:

- Change both the main object and the spatial arrangement between illustrations.
- Do not repeat the same composition pattern consecutively.
- Derive metaphors from the current article rather than a stock visual library.

## QA checklist

- metaphor is original
- line feels intentional
- awkwardness expresses the claim
- composition remains sparse
- image reads without text

Failure signals:

- looks like a generic doodle
- joke requires a caption
- too many objects
- line is merely sloppy
- metaphor is clichéd
