# Paper Architecture style guide

## Visual DNA

Architectural paper relief made from scored, folded, and cut stock in which a flat field physically becomes a route, chamber, arch, or working threshold.

- Aspect ratio: 16:9 horizontal
- Canvas: warm white tabletop or seamless paper field with real contact shadows and enough room to read the construction
- Rendering language: clean hand-cut edges, visible fold scores, layered paper thickness, restrained pencil registration, and soft directional light
- Minimum whitespace: 28%
- Maximum subject coverage: 68%
- Typography: No headline or decorative type; allow at most three supplied one-word annotations printed flat beside the structure.

## Palette

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette 1 | `#E6DED0` | primary structure and focal subject |
| Palette 2 | `#365A92` | supporting contrast and secondary forms |
| Palette 3 | `#CE684E` | accent for emphasis and directional cues |
| Palette 4 | `#D8B76C` | restrained supporting accent |

## Must

- construction can be understood
- flat-to-volume transformation
- one usable route
- paper thickness is visible
- shadows explain structure

## Never

- origami animal
- pop-up greeting card
- scrapbook
- architectural model showcase
- random folded sculpture
- paper confetti

## Negative constraints

- no logo
- no invented text
- no glossy plastic
- no intricate ornament
- no impossible folds
- no decorative cutouts without function

## Composition patterns

### Focal metaphor

Best for: core argument, abstract concept.

Build one dominant physical metaphor around the article's central relationship.

### Contrast

Best for: before and after, tradeoff, tension.

Use two clearly different states joined by one meaningful transition.

### Process

Best for: workflow, cause and effect, transformation.

Show only the essential stages and make the change itself visually dominant.

### System relationship

Best for: framework, network, components.

Limit the system to three to five meaningful parts with an obvious hierarchy.

### Narrative scene

Best for: human consequence, decision, editorial tension.

Stage one decisive moment rather than summarizing the whole article.

## Metaphor method

1. Translate the abstract relationship into a physical arrangement.
2. Choose one object family suited to the visual medium.
3. Make the decisive change visible without relying on a headline.

Object pool:

- folded arch
- cut route
- paper chamber
- raised bridge
- scored threshold
- layered platform
- hinged plane
- nested shelter

Action pool:

- fold
- raise
- open
- bridge
- cut through
- layer
- support
- reconfigure

Anti-repetition:

- Change both the main object and the spatial arrangement between illustrations.
- Do not repeat the same composition pattern consecutively.
- Derive metaphors from the current article rather than a stock visual library.

## QA checklist

- paper construction is plausible
- shadow clarifies depth
- route carries the concept
- material remains restrained
- thumbnail silhouette is strong

Failure signals:

- looks like decorative origami
- structure has no use
- too many folds
- shadow is artificial
- paper loses tactile quality
