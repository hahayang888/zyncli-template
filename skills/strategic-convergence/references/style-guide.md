# Strategic Convergence style guide

## Visual DNA

Elegant editorial strategy infographic using two balanced evidence zones, disciplined flowing ribbons, a central inflection point, sparse milestones, and restrained lavender-to-slate hierarchy.

- Aspect ratio: 16:9 horizontal
- Canvas: warm off-white information field with generous margins, a quiet horizontal timeline, and one central transformation spanning the composition
- Rendering language: precise thin rules, soft-edged vector ribbons, subtle grid discipline, small evidence modules, restrained lavender and cool-gray fills, and no decorative illustration
- Minimum whitespace: 25%
- Maximum subject coverage: 72%
- Typography: Use only 4-10 short real English labels supplied by the brief; maintain strict hierarchy and never generate paragraphs, filler copy, or pseudo-writing.

## Palette

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette 1 | `#6B72E8` | primary structure and focal subject |
| Palette 2 | `#A69AE8` | supporting contrast and secondary forms |
| Palette 3 | `#CBD0D8` | accent for emphasis and directional cues |
| Palette 4 | `#F4F1EC` | restrained supporting accent |

## Must

- one central inflection point
- clear left-to-right time logic
- several inputs converging
- several strategic outcomes expanding
- two balanced evidence zones
- one dominant numeric or proportional takeaway when supplied

## Never

- Sankey chart copied from a template
- PowerPoint slide frame
- dashboard chrome
- dense consulting slide
- decorative wave
- tiny unreadable text
- many colors
- icon library clutter

## Negative constraints

- no logo
- no fake statistics
- no invented dates
- no pseudo-writing
- no paragraph text
- no screenshot UI
- no 3D effects
- no stock icons
- no decorative gradient background

## Composition patterns

### Focal metaphor

Best for: core argument, abstract concept.

Build one dominant physical metaphor around the article's central relationship.

### Contrast

Best for: before and after, tradeoff, tension.

Use two clearly different states joined by one meaningful transition.

### Process

Best for: workflow, cause and effect, transformation.

Show only the essential stages and make the change itself visually dominant.

### System relationship

Best for: framework, network, components.

Limit the system to three to five meaningful parts with an obvious hierarchy.

### Narrative scene

Best for: human consequence, decision, editorial tension.

Stage one decisive moment rather than summarizing the whole article.

## Metaphor method

1. Translate the abstract relationship into a physical arrangement.
2. Choose one object family suited to the visual medium.
3. Make the decisive change visible without relying on a headline.

Object pool:

- converging ribbons
- inflection marker
- paired evidence columns
- timeline rail
- outcome nodes
- input modules
- proportion bars
- decision seam

Action pool:

- converge
- shift
- transform
- expand
- prioritize
- reallocate
- connect
- measure

Anti-repetition:

- Change both the main object and the spatial arrangement between illustrations.
- Do not repeat the same composition pattern consecutively.
- Derive metaphors from the current article rather than a stock visual library.

## QA checklist

- reading order is immediate
- inflection point dominates
- labels are real and concise
- ribbons encode meaning rather than decoration
- evidence zones remain balanced
- graphic works at article width

Failure signals:

- looks like a generic consulting slide
- text is unreadable
- ribbons are decorative
- too many modules
- timeline competes with the thesis
- invented statistics appear
