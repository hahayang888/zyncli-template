# Carved Thesis style guide

## Visual DNA

Forceful linocut or woodcut editorial scenes with monumental adult figures, carved tool marks, and one structural act that embodies a durable social argument.

- Aspect ratio: 16:9 horizontal
- Canvas: warm fibrous paper with a large carved field, irregular ink density, and open margins
- Rendering language: bold relief-print blacks, gouged highlights, simplified adult anatomy, compressed perspective, and one muted red or ochre plate
- Minimum whitespace: 21%
- Maximum subject coverage: 75%
- Typography: No headline, poster slogan, banner, protest sign, or fake carved lettering.

## Palette

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette 1 | `#18253C` | primary structure and focal subject |
| Palette 2 | `#B54B38` | supporting contrast and secondary forms |
| Palette 3 | `#C89A45` | accent for emphasis and directional cues |
| Palette 4 | `#E7DFCF` | restrained supporting accent |

## Must

- one collective structural action
- visible carved direction
- credible adult weight
- high-contrast hierarchy
- one supporting color plate

## Never

- propaganda poster
- folk-art imitation
- hero worship
- medieval woodcut pastiche
- comic action pose
- decorative printmaking

## Negative constraints

- no political logo
- no slogan
- no public figure
- no gore
- no detailed facial likeness
- no glossy digital shading

## Composition patterns

### Focal metaphor

Best for: core argument, abstract concept.

Build one dominant physical metaphor around the article's central relationship.

### Contrast

Best for: before and after, tradeoff, tension.

Use two clearly different states joined by one meaningful transition.

### Process

Best for: workflow, cause and effect, transformation.

Show only the essential stages and make the change itself visually dominant.

### System relationship

Best for: framework, network, components.

Limit the system to three to five meaningful parts with an obvious hierarchy.

### Narrative scene

Best for: human consequence, decision, editorial tension.

Stage one decisive moment rather than summarizing the whole article.

## Metaphor method

1. Translate the abstract relationship into a physical arrangement.
2. Choose one object family suited to the visual medium.
3. Make the decisive change visible without relying on a headline.

Object pool:

- carved arch
- shared beam
- raised platform
- blocked gate
- stone-like fragments
- support frame
- collective bridge
- open passage

Action pool:

- assemble
- lift
- support
- carve
- cross
- hold
- repair
- resist

Anti-repetition:

- Change both the main object and the spatial arrangement between illustrations.
- Do not repeat the same composition pattern consecutively.
- Derive metaphors from the current article rather than a stock visual library.

## QA checklist

- tool marks reinforce force
- figures carry real weight
- action expresses the claim
- print remains contemporary
- silhouette is legible

Failure signals:

- propaganda aesthetic
- stiff figures
- historic pastiche
- too much black loses structure
- color plate becomes decorative
